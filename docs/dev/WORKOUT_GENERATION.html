1. Overview

Every generated workout comes out of the same engine, planner.js. Two front doors lead to it. The week planner (the program editor) plans a whole week at once. A one-tap tile or a program's next day plans a single day, as a one-day week, through generateWorkout in generate.js.

Settings
Goals

Goal type, volume, focus per group, per-group overrides.

Settings
Constraints

Days a week, minutes a workout, equipment, level, rests, skipped lifts.

History
Logged workouts

Sets done this week, the last 3 weeks' shortfall, recent lifts, pace.

Library
Your list

Your exercises, plus library lifts for anything the list lacks.

Targets
Weekly sets per group and per part

weeklyTargetOf, partTargetOf (goals.js). For a single day: what is still owed, split across the days left (dayGoals / dayShare).

Engine
planner.js: weekOf then giveWay

Skeleton of compound slots → fill with accessories → top up → trim → (balance, week only). Then lower-tier groups give way if a higher tier fell short.

Output
Lifts with sets, reps, rest and RIR

Each pick becomes an entry with a weight from your history (buildPlannedEntry), or an inferred start weight.

How a part is credited

A lift credits a part 1 when the part's muscle is its target, 0.2 when it is a synergist. The group gets 1 and 0.4: a synergist counts twice as much toward its group as toward its part, because a group's goal is met by many parts at once. A lift aims at a part when that credit is at least 1. When a group falls behind, choose prefers a lift aimed at its most-short part (behind). The shoulders' accessory is always a side- or rear-delt isolation lift, whichever delt has had fewer lifts this week (deltLift, turnOf).

GapParts have shares but no tiers, and only three groups have planned parts. Front delts, traps, the abs' obliques, biceps' brachialis, triceps' long head, the glutes' medius and the calves' tibialis are never asked for on purpose. Stage 1 below adds a tiered part list for every group.

6. Taxonomy 4: equipment

Planner levels

EQUIPMENT: gym ⊃ db ⊃ bw. A library lift needs a kit within the level. Your own list is filtered by the active equipment profile (suggestable).

NO_LEAD: cable, machine and similar lifts can't lead a day.

Tool families

variant.js TOOL: barbell, dumbbell, smith, cable, machine, bodyweight, kettlebell, band, other. Body positions are read from the name. KITS (facets) fold them slightly differently.

Catalogue counts

body weight 2246, dumbbell 757, cable 446, barbell 389, leverage machine 280, band 261, kettlebell 187, … of 5,632 rows.

Equivalents

Tiers: lift (same lift, other kit) → move → muscle. Used by the swap sheet, not by the planner.

What rotates equipment today: novel asks for a different tool for the same group on the same day; variant prefers the lift done longest ago. Neither tracks which kit a movement used last time.

GapNo rotation of kit within a movement across the week. Stage 3. Stage 0 added two kits: Plate (weight plate, 45 rows) and Ball (medicine, stability and bosu ball, 133). 8. Workflow: one-tap day

generateWorkout(S, type, setup) in generate.js. Runs when you tap Full / Push / Pull / Legs, open the setup sheet, or shuffle.

1 · Inputs
Setup and constraints

genSetupOf (ticked groups, minutes or count, sets/reps by kind, warm-ups) + genConstraints (goal, level, equipment, minutes).

2 · Pool
What may be picked
  • planPool: your list minus cardio, stretches, skipped and off-profile lifts; Olympic lifts and skills only if starred.
  • The planner adds library lifts for any movement, group or part the list lacks.
  • trainingIndex: when each lift and muscle was last trained.
3 · Goals for today
dayGoals
  • lack(g) = weekly target − sets done this week (calendar week in a program, else last 7 days).
  • Split over the days left that train g: lack / daysFor(g).
  • Catch up: × (1 + 0.5 × the average shortfall of the last 3 weeks), never over lack.
  • Part goals scale with their group's.
  • On a program day, the program's share replaces this split (§9).
4 · Lead
leadFor

Which pattern leads today, and which lift (§12). Lifts done in the last session are kept off (near), the two before are de-preferred (used).

5 · Daily groups
dailyGroups (full days only)

Owed daily groups, most-missed first then by tier. Each gets an aimed accessory before the rest of the fill.

6 · Plan the day
planDay → weekOf (1 day) → giveWay

Skeleton, fill, top up, trim (§10); then fit to the exercise count if untimed; then give way (§11).

7 · Fill the time
fillMinutes

Rebuild at goals × k for k in 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4 (each group at least (k−1)×2 sets, more accessories allowed). Stop within 5 min of the time, or when a step runs over or adds nothing. Short is still measured against the k=1 goals.

8 · Shape
Slots, abs combo, extras
  • Slot per pick: lead / main / acc / small, with any extra sets.
  • turnAbs: every other abs day is a 4-exercise body-weight combo; otherwise prefer a weighted abs lift.
  • Optional: 1 cardio, 2 stretches.
9 · Entries
generatedEntries

Sets, rep range, rest in your range, warm-up rows, weight from your history.

Gap: never-logged lifts show 0 lbA lift you have never logged can come out with no weight. The forecast at 8a31a86d0 showed 0 lb for a front pulldown, an incline fly, a face pull and a barbell shrug. Stage 4 seeds a first weight from an equivalent you have logged; until then the inferred start weight should at least never be 0 for a loaded lift. Issue W in §15.

9. Workflow: program day

A program (program.js) stores the plan's settings (goal, level, equipment, days, minutes, volume) and a day order. Each day's lifts are still picked fresh by generateWorkout; the stored week is only what the editor showed.

1 · Which day
programRounds / programForecast

Sessions since the start fill the first open day of their kind. Days are 2 apart when there are 4 or fewer, else 1; a new week starts at least 7 days after the last.

2 · This day's share
dayShare

For each group the day trains: share = min(lack ÷ days left this week that train it, even split × most), where even = goal ÷ program days that train it, most = 1.5 (1 in the first week).

3 · Orphans
adoptedShare

A group the program trains but no remaining day this week does gets its floor minus what is done (if ≥ 1). It is not scaled by fillMinutes. Not in the first week.

4 · Size
sizeDay / plan minutes

Timed when the plan has minutes (the sheet's own minutes are for that workout only). Untimed: build uncapped, then clamp to 3–15 exercises (a combo counts as one).

5 · Build
generateWorkout with share

As §8 from step 2, with the program's day order as the cycle and the lead counted from the program's start.

6 · Forecast
planAhead

Builds the next N days in turn, logging each as a synthetic workout so later days see it. Drives the planned-day cards and the week panel.

10. Workflow: the planner's passes

weekOf in planner.js. The program editor runs it for the whole week; planDay runs it for a single day.

Pass 1
Skeleton

Each day gets its lead, then its compound slots by day type (lower / push / pull), sets paced by need. Over time? Drop sets to the slot minimum, then the lead to 3, then the slot nearest its goal.

Pass 2
Fill

First the daily loop (full days), then the accessory loop: score groups by need = gap ÷ days left; skip any blocked by time, caps (accessories a day, sets a session, one-a-day) or equipment; pick a lift with choose.

Pass 3
Top up

Add sets (or another accessory) where a group or part is still short and there is room.

Pass 4
Trim

Cut sets from groups over their goal.

Pass 5 (week only)
Balance

Move accessories or sets between days to even out the minutes.

choose: which lift fills a group

  1. far: not on an adjacent day (or the last session).
  2. novel: no third compound of one pattern; alternate pull direction; a different tool for the same group.
  3. behind: aimed at the group's most-short part.
  4. turnOf: for shoulders, the delt with fewer lifts this week.
  5. helps: credits the group (target before synergist).
  6. variant: kept → known (logged in 56 days) → done longest ago → popular → seeded random, then offset by shuffle.

Caps (ADV, LEVELS)

Accessories a day: 3 / 5 / 6 by level. Sets of one group in a session: 10. Direct lifts for a group a day: 2 (1 for one-a-day groups). Seconds a rep: 3. Warm-up: 5 min flat. Between lifts: 30 s.

Gap: groups overshoot their goal, and abs fall shortIn the forecast at 8a31a86d0 (an example lifter's data, three program days), most groups landed 10–25% over their weekly goal and shoulders at 19.6 of 12, nearly all from presses and rows crediting them as a synergist. Trim can only cut sets that are the group's own, and cutting a press to trim shoulders would short the chest. Abs were the one group under goal, at 10.8 of 12. Not re-measured since de81987a3. Issue V in §15; stage 1's part tiers should help, since a part already covered by compounds won't ask for a lift of its own.

giveWay: when the time is tight, lower tiers cut back so higher tiers reach their goals.

Start
The full build

Try
Scale that tier's goals

Tier 3: ×0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0. Tiers 2 and 1: ×0.75, 0.5. Rebuild (top up, trim, balance).

Yes

Continue with the next scale or tier.

No

A group is "short" when it is below 85% of its goal (SHORT). shortOf / causesOf label why: time, accessories, equipment, caps, template, or planner.

12. The lead lift

leadFor (generate.js) and leadPattern (engine.js).

Which pattern

Non-full days cycle their patterns: push = hpress, vpress; pull = vpull, hpull; legs/lower = squat, hinge; upper = all four. A full day alternates a lower lead (squat, hinge) with an upper lead that moves on each 28-day block. A pattern whose muscle was trained yesterday is skipped.

Which lift

Candidates: logged compound lifts (stalest first), then starred, then staples. Rotation session: least recently done wins. Rotation block: the most used in the last 56 days, held for the block. The lead must be in your pool.

Gap: weak main liftsNothing ties a main slot to how hard a lift is, or to your level. The forecast at 8a31a86d0 had:
  • a cable low chest press first on Friday (check why NO_LEAD let a cable lift lead),
  • a renegade row as the main back lift,
  • an assisted kneeling dip as the chest lift for an advanced lifter,
  • a reverse curl, mostly a forearm lift (moveOf files it under wrist), as the biceps lift.
Lead and main slots need a loadable compound that suits the level, and an accessory for a group must target that group, not only its neighbour. Issue L in §15.

14. How time is estimated

A set = average reps × 3 s (× 2 if per side) + rest. With a rest range, capped at your measured pace + 30 s; else your pace replaces it when known.

Your pace (pace.js): active time ÷ sets in a workout (gaps over 10 min ignored, at least 8 sets); the median of the last 10 workouts, once there are 3.

A workout = 5 min warm-up + the sum of its sets. The planner uses this to trim and fill; the sheet and the forecast show the same sum (minutesFor) from the built entries.

Known inaccuraciesWarm-up sets aren't timed one by one, only the flat 5 min. The abs combo's 4 members are each timed at their own rest, not the combo's shared 60 s, and the planner timed one abs lift before the combo replaced it. Cardio and stretches count toward the minutes. The planner's own estimate runs about a minute under what the app then shows for the built day, so program.test.js's length tolerance was loosened by a minute in de81987a3 rather than fixed. Issue T in §15.

1

A tiered part list for every group

Each group lists its muscles with a tier and a share, e.g. shoulders: side 1, rear 1, front 2; chest: mid/lower 1, upper 1; back: lats 1, mid back 1, traps 2, lower back 3; abs: rectus 1, obliques 2; biceps: biceps 1, brachialis 2; triceps: long head 1. The planner asks for tier-1 parts before tier-2 ones, the way groups give way today, and counts what compounds already gave a part before giving it a lift of its own. Where the labels have no heads (quads, hamstrings; §4), a part is defined by its move: leg extension for the rectus femoris, hinge vs leg curl. The calves have theirs in the roles now (gastrocnemius, soleus). Replaces PLANNED_PARTS / PART_SHARES.

Built so far: the turns decided in §16 (familyOf, dueFamily, smallTurn in planner.js, tested in planner.test.js under "taking turns within a group"). They need your history, so a first workout plans as before. Shoulders go side, rear, side, overhead press; the press counts as the day's shoulder lift on its turn. The chest's lead press and push slot take the half done longest ago (upper, or mid and lower), and a fly on the same day stays in that half, so the next workout takes the other. The abs go flexion, rotation, bracing. A day whose lifts have no hinge gets a leg curl for the hamstrings. A full day takes one small-muscle pair, calves and traps or leg extension and forearms, at their fewest sets and within the level's accessory count; calves alternate standing and seated through the move rotation. Still open: tiers and shares for every group's parts, and counting what compounds gave a part before giving it a lift.

2

Movement rotation within a part

Use the 50 MOVES already in exercise-facets.js: a part's week mixes its moves (press and fly for chest; pulldown and pullover for lats), picking the move done least recently.

Done. With your history, variant ranks a slot's lifts by the day their move was last done, then the day the lift was, one never done first, so a rear-delt fly follows a face pull and every lift on your list comes round.

3

Equipment rotation within a movement

Within a move, rotate the kit (barbell, dumbbell, machine, cable, kettlebell…), only from your own list and equipment profile.

Done. Of the lifts of the move that won, one on a kit other than last time's, at random, and no kit takes more than half the day's lifts. A kit with only one lift on your list keeps it.

4

Personal ratios and a trend per movement

Learn your own ratios between equivalent lifts (e.g. trap-bar vs barbell deadlift) once each has been logged; seed a first weight from an equivalent; track progress per movement rather than per exact lift. A strength goal keeps its main lifts fixed. A lift's first session isn't a true baseline: part of the second session's jump is learning the lift, so the trend shouldn't read it as strength gained.

16. Target template

Where the planner should get to. This is the same example program as today's planner run (3 full-body days, 75 minutes, the example's weekly goals), built by hand from the rules below, mostly from lifts the example lifter has already logged. Each column is one workout; each row is a slot that every workout has, so the days line up and you can read across a row to see the rotation. The sets are hidden from the cells but counted in the result block, which uses the app's own crediting (plannedSets).

1. Same slots every day. Four compounds (lead, lower, push, pull), then one lift each for shoulders, biceps, triceps and abs, calves on two days, then one need lift. A day never gets three back lifts because the week happened to be short on lats.
2. Rotation has levels. Within a week, a slot changes the part or movement it trains (side → rear delt, curl → hammer). Across weeks, it changes the variation or equipment (barbell → dumbbell → Smith). The lead stays the same for the block so it can progress.
3. Credit first, then direct work. The compounds' credit is counted before the daily slots are sized. That's why shoulders and triceps get 2 sets, not 3–5. The overhead press counts here too: when it is the shoulders' turn, its front-delt and triceps credit comes off the other slots first.
4. One need slot. After the template, a single lift goes to the group furthest behind its weekly goal. In this program that's the hamstrings twice and the back once.

What the target can't fix: shoulders. Even at 2 sets a day of side and rear delts, shoulders reach 16 of 12, because presses and rows count 0.4 a set toward them: about 10 sets a week before any lift of their own. Cutting the daily lift would put them on goal on paper but leave the side and rear delts with nothing. (Triceps had the same problem until the bench day's push became a fly, which credits no triceps.) So the goal itself has to change, not the workouts. A group's goal should count direct work for the parts compounds barely reach (side delts, triceps long head), as stage 1's part goals propose (§15).
The biceps heads, fixed in the data. Stage 0 now labels the biceps heads, as it did the triceps: each of the 208 curls was read and given a target by where the arm sits. Arm behind the body, a drag or a close grip: long head (33, the incline and drag curls). Arm in front, on a pad or overhead, or a wide grip: short head (76, the preacher, spider, concentration and cable overhead curls). A standard curl with the elbows at the sides stays biceps, both heads evenly (81). The table with every decision and its reason is scripts/catalogue/roles/biceps-heads.tsv. Each week now has one curl for each head (a preacher curl beside an incline curl, or a close-grip curl beside a preacher) and a hammer curl for the brachialis.
How today's planner differs (its forecast is below).
  • Slots change from day to day: the need loop adds extras by part (a shrug for upper traps, a pulldown for lats), so one day has three back lifts and another two chest lifts.
  • Compound credit isn't counted before the daily slots are sized, so shoulders run 30–50% over.
  • Rotation happens only through variant names and equipment. The push slot is a press every day and the pull slot a row every day; nothing rotates parts within a week.
  • Some days skip their daily shoulder lift.
